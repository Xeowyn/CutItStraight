## Long description

> A plank feeds into a running scroll saw, and a line is drawn down it. Your job is
> simple to say and hard to do: keep the blade on that line.
>
> The blade is bolted down — the **board** is what you steer. Drag left and right to
> swing the timber under the teeth as the line curves, kinks and doubles back. Hold
> the line and your combo climbs to 5x. Drift and the board starts to split. Cut it
> perfectly and you earn a fourth, purple star.
>
> **Forty-eight boards** to cut: pine and walnut, orange padauk, blood-red bloodwood,
> purpleheart, striped zebrano, spalted maple, walnut burl, charred pine, painted barn
> board — then steel, copper, gold, Damascus steel, tread plate and solid ice, each in
> its own workshop and each with its own sound.
>
> A hundred levels, and almost every one is different: short sprints, hard angles,
> narrow stock, fine tolerances, and boards that arrive **in pieces**, where you tap to
> jump the saw across the gap. Every twist is written up on a work order before the
> board moves, so you always know what you're in for.
>
> Old coins, pocket watches, screws and horseshoes turn up buried in the timber — saw
> through them for a bonus. Nail a hard corner and you earn a key; three keys open a
> treasure chest.
>
> Spend what you earn on the saw itself: eight parts, four tiers each. Tungsten teeth
> widen your margin, a geared motor gives you more thinking time, a guide laser warns
> you before a hard corner, dust extraction keeps the board from splitting — and four
> more that change what a good cut is worth. Every part changes how the saw plays, and
> every part can be switched off again.
>
> Or furnish the rooms: a woodshop, a kitchen, a living room and a music room, built
> piece by piece with the timber you cut — and every finished room stays there to
> look back at.
>
> Finish the hundredth board to unlock **Endless**: pick the feed speed and the line
> complexity yourself, the boards come in a fresh shuffled order every run, and the
> harder you set it the more each board pays. There's a Daily Cut with a streak to
> keep, a free daily chest, and jobs from Old Mac when you want a target.

## How to play / controls

> **Drag left and right** anywhere on the screen to steer the board under the blade.
> **Arrow keys**, **WASD** or **ZQSD** also work. When the board comes in pieces,
> **tap** (or press **Space**) to jump the saw across the gap.
>
> Keep the blade on the line. The glow around the line is your margin — stay inside it
> for a perfect cut. Damage builds while you're off the line and repairs itself while
> you're back on it, so a wobble is recoverable; a long drift is not.
>
> - **SPLIT meter** — the bar under the level progress. It fills when you are off the
>   line and empties when you are back on it. When it fills, the board is firewood.
> - **Main menu** — everything in the game is on it: play, level select, Endless, the
>   build bench, saw upgrades and settings.
> - **Gear icon** — pause, level select, the build rooms, saw upgrades, separate
>   sound-effects and music volume, and inverted steering, at any moment including
>   mid-cut.
> - **Invert steering** is in that menu if you read the board as the thing you push
>   rather than the thing the world moves under.

Controls listed for the portal's structured field:

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Mouse drag / touch drag | Steer the board |
| ← / → , A / D , Q / D | Steer the board |

## Category

**Puzzle.**

Not Arcade, not Casual, and the reason is measurable rather than aesthetic: the
platform's published average playtime by genre puts Puzzle at 21 min against 8.6 for
hypercasual, and average playtime is one of the three metrics Basic Launch is judged
on. The game supports the claim honestly — boards are read-ahead-and-plan, not
reflex — so this is not a mis-tag.

If Puzzle is unavailable, second choice is **Skill**, third is **Casual**.
